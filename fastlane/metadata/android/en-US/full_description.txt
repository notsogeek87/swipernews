SwiperNews shows your RSS feeds full screen, one article per card, swiped vertically like a social media timeline — with no algorithm, no ads and no trackers. The app only contacts the RSS feeds you pick yourself, plus Wikipedia's public API for the Learn mode.

Main features:

• Full-screen vertical swipe navigation (or arrow keys / space bar)
• Add, remove and toggle your RSS sources
• Import and export your sources as OPML or JSON
• Reading position remembered per source, right where you left off
• Articles open in a built-in browser, dark and without an address bar: you never leave the app, and going back returns to the card you came from — articles can also be opened in your usual browser instead
• Reader mode, e-reader style: title, text and images only, in a comfortable column on a dark background — one button switches back to the full page at any time
• Immersive reading: the reader bar slides away as soon as you scroll down, and cookie banners are hidden — hidden, never accepted: no consent is ever given on your behalf (can be turned off)
• Ad and tracker blocking, on if you want it (off by default): requests are cut before they are even loaded, for lighter pages and less tracking. A public blocklist (EasyList, StevenBlack) can then be downloaded in one tap and keeps itself up to date — nothing is downloaded until you have chosen a source
• Share an article to the app of your choice
• Learn mode: random Wikipedia articles to swipe through, filtered by interests (science, history, art, geography, nature, space, technology, sport, cinema, films, music, video games, artists, cooking, philosophy…)
• Works offline once the app has been loaded

Note: the app interface is currently in French only.

SwiperNews is free software (MIT licence). The source code is fully available, and the app requests no permission beyond the Internet access needed to fetch the feeds.
